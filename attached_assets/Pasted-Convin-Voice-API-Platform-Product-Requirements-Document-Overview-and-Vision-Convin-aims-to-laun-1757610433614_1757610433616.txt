Overview and Vision
Convin aims to launch a Voice AI Platform offering developers and enterprises robust Speech-to-Text (STT)
and Text-to-Speech (TTS) capabilities via cloud APIs. This platform will leverage Convin’s in-house AI
expertise (including a custom LLM) to deliver high-accuracy transcription and natural-sounding speech
synthesis. The goal is to combine the strengths of industry benchmarks like Deepgram (for STT) and
ElevenLabs (for TTS) into a single easy-to-use, enterprise-ready service. Initially, the platform’s scope is
focused only on STT and TTS APIs, ensuring we do these core functions exceptionally well before
expanding. By providing flexible deployment options (cloud by default, with on-premises/vPrivate Cloud for
enterprise needs) and an intuitive user experience, Convin’s Voice API Platform will empower businesses to
integrate voice capabilities quickly and at scale. The vision is to support a range of use cases – from real￾time call transcription in contact centers to generating lifelike voiceovers – all while adhering to Convin’s
high standards of quality, security, and design.
Key Objectives: The platform should deliver best-in-class accuracy and speed for transcription, and highly
realistic, expressive speech synthesis. It must offer a developer-friendly experience (clear documentation,
SDKs, sample code, and a web console) to help users build voice-enabled apps faster. It should also meet
enterprise requirements for scalability, reliability, and data privacy. Ultimately, Convin’s Voice API Platform
will position the company as a one-stop solution for voice AI – “the voice OS for developers” akin to what
Deepgram markets – enabling voice-first applications with minimal effort.
Target Users and Use Cases
The platform will cater to two broad user groups: enterprise clients and individual developers/startups
(with a focus on enterprise readiness). In the spirit of Deepgram’s flexibility to serve both startups and the
world’s top enterprises , Convin’s offering will scale from free trials to large deployments:
Enterprises (Primary Focus): Enterprises can use the STT API for contact center call transcription,
sales call analysis, meeting transcription, and compliance monitoring. These users need high
accuracy, data security, and possibly on-premise deployments for sensitive data. For example, a BPO
or bank’s call center can stream calls to our API and get real-time transcripts with speaker labels and
alerts (for keywords or sentiment). Enterprises might also use the TTS API to power voice assistants,
IVR systems, or automated alerts in a consistent brand voice. They require reliability, SLAs, and
integration with their existing systems.
Developers & Startups: Tech startups or individual developers might integrate our voice APIs into
their apps – e.g. a mobile app adding voice notes transcription, an e-learning platform generating
audio lessons, or a game using TTS for character dialogue. They value ease of use, affordability (free

Platform Features and Requirements
The Convin Voice API Platform will consist of two core API offerings (STT and TTS) and a suite of supporting
platform features. Below, we detail the requirements for each component:
Speech-to-Text (STT) API Features
Real-Time Streaming Transcription: The STT API will offer low-latency streaming transcription over
secure WebSocket or gRPC. This allows applications to send audio in real-time and receive
incremental transcripts (word-by-word or sentence-by-sentence) with minimal delay. The target
latency is sub-300 milliseconds from speech to text, to enable conversational use cases . For
example, in a live agent assist scenario, the platform can transcribe a customer’s words almost
instantly, enabling real-time prompts. This real-time mode should handle streaming audio with
continuous processing and return partial results that update as confidence improves.
Batch/Async Transcription: In addition to streaming, the API will support transcription of pre￾recorded audio files (async mode). Users can submit audio (in formats like WAV, MP3, FLAC) via a
REST API and either poll for results or receive a callback/webhook when the transcript is ready. This
mode is vital for processing voicemails, long recordings, or bulk workloads. The system should
handle large files (at least up to a couple of hours in duration) and provide timestamps for words
or sentences in the result JSON.
Accuracy and NLP Enhancements: The transcription engine should achieve very high accuracy
(target >90% accuracy under ideal conditions ) using state-of-the-art speech recognition models.
It will include “smart formatting” to enhance readability: automatic punctuation, capitalization, and
even formatting of common constructs like dates or abbreviations. For example, the raw output
“we’ll meet at five pm on september first” would be smart-formatted as “We’ll meet at 5 PM on

Custom Vocabulary & Context Biasing: Users should be able to supply a list of custom terms
(proper names, jargon, product names) that the model might not recognize by default. The STT API
will support keyword boosting or key term prompting, where developers can hint important
words to improve their recall . For instance, a company name or technical term can be specified
so the transcript is more likely to get it right. This feature helps reach enterprise-grade accuracy for
domain-specific audio (similar to Deepgram’s keyword boosting which improves key phrase recall up
to 90% ). Initially this may be a simple biasing mechanism; in future, full custom model training
on proprietary data is envisioned (an enterprise feature – see below).
Speaker Diarization: The transcription service will be able to detect multiple speakers in an audio
stream and label segments by speaker (e.g. Speaker 1, Speaker 2) . This is essential for meeting
transcripts or call center calls. The API should return speaker labels for each transcript segment or
even each word (like “Speaker A: [text]”). Note: If true identity of speakers is needed (speaker
recognition), that is a complex feature likely out-of-scope for v1 – we will limit to differentiating
speakers. Diarization can be turned on/off via an API parameter to save costs when not needed
(similar to how some APIs charge extra for diarization due to complexity).
Filler Word Control: The STT engine will offer an option to handle filler words (“um”, “uh”, stutters).
By default, fillers may be omitted for a cleaner transcript, but if enabled, the API will include them to
provide a verbatim transcript . This flexibility is useful; for example, in analysis of speech patterns
or in legal transcripts, every utterance including fillers might be required, whereas for readability
they’re usually dropped.
Profanity Filtering / Redaction: The platform will include a profanity filter option to mask or bleep
offensive language in transcripts. More generally, it will support redaction of sensitive data . For
instance, if credit card numbers or personal identifiers are spoken, the API can detect and replace
them with “[REDACTED]” in the text. This feature is crucial for compliance in industries like healthcare
(HIPAA) or finance, and mirrors Deepgram’s redaction feature for privacy . Initially, simple regex￾based or model-based detection for phone numbers, SSN, curse words, etc., will be provided.
Multi-Language Support: To reach a broad market, the STT service will not be limited to English. We
plan support for multiple languages and dialects. Deepgram’s STT covers 36+ languages , and
Azure’s spans over 140 – we aim to start with the most needed languages (e.g. English, Spanish,
possibly Hindi and other Indian languages relevant to Convin’s clients) and expand gradually. The
API design should allow specifying language (e.g. ?lang=en-US vs en-GB for dialects). In the
future, auto language detection can be added to automatically route audio to the correct model.
Note: Non-English models may initially be slightly less accurate; we will leverage open-source
models or partner to bootstrap multi-lingual capability quickly, given it’s a high-value feature
(ElevenLabs and others highlight multilingual support as key ).

includes the transcript text and segments with timestamps (start/end time for each word or

Performance & Accuracy Benchmarks: While building the product, we will benchmark our STT

Scalability & Rate Limits: The STT service must handle high concurrency for enterprise use (think
hundreds of simultaneous call streams). The backend will use scalable microservices and GPU
acceleration to process audio in real-time. An incoming audio stream will be chunked and distributed
as needed to avoid bottlenecks. We’ll define rate limits or quotas per API key (e.g. X simultaneous
streams for free tier) to protect service stability, with higher limits for paid tiers. The system should
auto-scale in the cloud to meet usage spikes. Additionally, we will provide client SDKs that manage
reconnection or buffering seamlessly in case of network hiccups, making real-time usage robust.
In summary, the STT API will provide developers with a highly accurate transcription service that includes all
the must-have features (like punctuation, speaker labeling, custom words, etc.) out-of-the-box . This
ensures minimal post-processing and easy integration of the output into their applications.
Text-to-Speech (TTS) API Features
High-Quality Neural Voices: The TTS API will produce speech using advanced neural speech
synthesis, ensuring output that is natural and human-like in sound. The platform should launch
with a library of diverse voices covering multiple genders, ages, and styles. For example, we might
offer at least 4–6 voices in English at launch (e.g. a friendly female voice, a warm male voice, a
narratorial voice, etc.), with plans to expand. These voices should be on par with industry leaders –
ElevenLabs is known for exceptionally lifelike voices , and Deepgram’s new Aura TTS boasts
extremely natural prosody for enterprise use . We aim for similar quality: clear pronunciation,
correct intonation, and minimal robotic artefacts even for long-form speech.
Expressiveness and Customization: A standout feature will be the ability to customize the speech
output in terms of tone and style. The API (and UI) should allow parameters or tags to adjust speech
speed, pitch, and emotion. For instance, developers can set a slower speed for a solemn narration
or a cheerful tone for a greeting. We’ll support standard SSML (Speech Synthesis Markup
Language) input so that users can finely control pronunciation (for acronyms or phonetic spelling),
add pauses, or emphasize words. (Support for SSML is critical for enterprise TTS; e.g. Amazon Polly
emphasizes their SSML support for precise control .) Additionally, we plan to incorporate an
“Expressive TTS” capability: possibly offering pre-defined styles (like “newsreader”, “excited”,

Multi-Lingual and Accent Support: The TTS system will be multilingual, allowing text input in
multiple languages to be spoken in the appropriate language. We will provide voices in at least the
major languages that we support for STT (for example, English, Spanish, Hindi, etc.). In cases where

We’ll also consider regional accents: e.g. different English accents (US, UK, Indian) so that
enterprises can choose a voice that best fits their audience. The platform’s aim is to connect with
global audiences through native-sounding speech . Text in a given language will require either
specifying the language code or auto-detecting it (the API could auto-detect language of the text to
choose the correct model). Initially, focus is on English (as a baseline) and one or two additional
languages; more can be added incrementally.
Low Latency, Real-Time Synthesis: Our TTS should be optimized for real-time applications. That
means achieving very low time-to-first-byte (TTFB) and fast synthesis, possibly streaming out audio
as it’s generated. Deepgram’s Aura-2, for example, achieves sub-200ms TTFB for enterprise TTS .
We will set a similar performance target so that even interactive voice agents can use our TTS
without noticeable delay. This may involve chunking long text or using faster “fast speech” models.
For typical sentence-length text, the API should return audio in a second or two at most. Being
“responsive” is crucial if this TTS is to be used in telephony/IVR or assistive devices. The service will
likely default to JSON or REST POST input and return an audio file (or audio stream). We might allow
a streaming websocket where text can be sent and audio streamed back for truly low-latency
pipelines (this is advanced – could be a future addition if not immediate).
Audio Format and Quality Options: The TTS output will be available in common audio formats
(initially 16 kHz PCM WAV for highest quality, and optionally compressed like MP3 or OGG for
convenience). Sample rate and audio format can be parameters. By default, we’ll produce broadcast￾quality audio suitable for production use. There may be an option to vary speaking speed globally
(e.g. 0.8x slower or 1.2x faster) if not using SSML, for quick adjustments. Volume normalization will
be handled so all voices have consistent loudness. The API should return audio length as metadata
as well (so apps know duration).
Voice Cloning & Custom Voices: A highly innovative feature on our roadmap is Instant Voice
Cloning – allowing a user to create a custom TTS voice from a few sample recordings (e.g. 1-5
minutes of audio of a person). This capability, akin to ElevenLabs’ Voice Lab and Microsoft’s Custom
Neural Voice, is not common in all platforms and would differentiate us . Initially, this might
be offered to enterprise customers or as a controlled beta, since training a new voice model is
compute-intensive. The idea is that a company could clone their brand voice (such as their IVR voice
talent) or an individual user could clone their own voice for personal use. Voice cloning should be
secured and consent-based to prevent abuse – for example, require explicit recordings from the
voice owner and perhaps verification. If shipping this feature fast is challenging, we may mark it as a
Phase 2 enhancement, but the platform design should accommodate it (e.g. having a concept of
user-created voices in the system, and allowing selection of those voices via the API or UI). Even

Voice Library Management: In the web console, users will be able to browse available voices with

Pronunciation and Domain Adaptation: To appeal to enterprise needs, our TTS should handle
domain-specific vocabulary well. This means things like spelling out alphanumeric codes, correctly
saying technical terms, and handling homographs via context. Deepgram’s enterprise TTS
emphasizes domain-specific pronunciation (like drug names or legal terms) . We can leverage our
in-house LLM to help with this: for example, the LLM could read the input text and provide hints or
phonetic spellings for unusual words before synthesis. As a result, our TTS will “sound right the
first time” even with complex jargon . Users might also be allowed to provide an IPA
pronunciation or correct mispronunciations via the SSML phoneme tag if needed.
Volume & Scalability: The TTS API must handle high volumes (potentially millions of characters
synthesized per day for big clients). The pricing model likely will be per character. We should
implement efficient batching on the backend to fully utilize GPUs (synthesizing multiple sentences in
parallel when possible). Also, rate limiting per minute may be in place for free tiers to prevent
abuse (e.g. someone trying to generate hours of audio on a free account). Enterprise customers will
expect that they can generate audio at scale (the system should scale horizontally). If required,
offering on-premises TTS (the model running in their environment for data privacy) might be
considered just like STT (Deepgram’s enterprise runtime can deploy TTS on prem ).
Monitoring and Feedback: To continuously improve, we will gather feedback on TTS outputs.
Possibly provide a mechanism for users to flag if a synthesis had an error (mispronounced word,
unnatural phrase) so we can address it in model updates. Our in-house LLM might even be used to
automatically evaluate TTS quality by “listening” to the audio and comparing to the input text to
catch issues (an innovative internal QC step).
Conclusion for TTS: The TTS API will offer lightning-fast, human-like speech generation with ample
control for users . By combining a wide voice selection, fine-grained customization, and future-forward
features like voice cloning, we’ll cater to a range of needs from dynamic IVR prompts to expressive content
creation. Our emphasis on enterprise (consistency, correct pronunciation of business terms, scalable
performance) will set us apart from purely entertainment-focused TTS engines .

Developer Console & API Platform Capabilities
Beyond the core AI models, a major part of this product is the developer platform enabling users to access
and manage the APIs easily. Key components and features of the platform include:
Cloud Portal (Web Console): A secure web application where users can sign up, manage their
account, and use dashboard functionalities. This console will be the hub for API consumers –
providing access to API keys, usage metrics, billing info, documentation, and tools like the
Playground (see Frontend Design section for UI details). The console should support single sign-on
(SSO) for enterprise customers (so that enterprise teams can log in via their corporate SSO if
needed), though basic email/password and OAuth options will be default for self-serve sign-up.
API Authentication & Keys: The platform will use API keys or tokens for authenticating API
requests. Upon sign-up, each user (or project) gets a unique API key. In the console, API Key
Management features will allow users to create multiple keys (to segregate projects or
environments), revoke/regenerate keys, and scope keys (e.g. maybe separate keys for STT vs TTS or
with usage limits). Keys will be long random strings or JWTs; all API calls must include the key in a
header (or use OAuth2 token in future for enterprise integrations). We will enforce HTTPS for all
endpoints.
Documentation & SDKs: Comprehensive developer documentation will be accessible from the
platform. This includes quickstart guides, API reference (endpoints, parameters, response formats),
and example code snippets in popular languages (cURL, Python, JavaScript, etc.). We plan to also
provide official SDKs or libraries to consume our API in at least a few languages (for example, a
Python package convin_voice and a Node.js package). These SDKs will wrap the REST calls or
streaming logic, making integration simpler (abstracting away auth and streaming protocols). Good
docs and SDKs are critical to reduce the friction for adoption – our standard should match or exceed
the clarity of Deepgram’s and ElevenLabs’ docs. We may cite usage examples in docs showing how to
transcribe audio in 5 lines of code, emphasizing the “build quickly” ethos .
Interactive API Playground: We will include a Playground tool in the web console (and possibly a
mini version on the marketing site homepage) to let users try out the STT and TTS easily. For STT, the
playground might allow uploading an audio file or even live-recording audio via the browser
microphone, then calling the API in the background to show the transcription result on screen (with
speaker labels and timestamps if enabled). For TTS, the playground will let the user input text,
choose a voice and style settings, and generate speech audio right there (with playback controls).
This interactive demo not only helps users validate quality before integration but also serves as a
sales demo (Deepgram’s site offers a similar live transcribe demo , and ElevenLabs has a web
interface for generating speech). Having a one-click tryout lowers the barrier for new users – they
can experience the API output without writing any code. The playground design and functionality is
detailed in the Frontend section below.
Usage Dashboard and Analytics: Users (especially enterprises) need visibility into how the APIs are
being used. The platform will feature a Dashboard with real-time and historical usage metrics: e.g.
minutes of audio transcribed this month, characters of TTS synthesized, number of API calls made,
etc. Graphs and charts will help visualize usage trends over time (daily/weekly). We will also break
down usage by type (STT vs TTS) and possibly by sub-project or key. This is analogous to an “Analytics

Logging and Debugging Tools: To aid developers during integration, the platform could provide a

wrong parameter. We might also show partial transcripts in logs for recent tasks (with PII redacted).
However, storing full content might raise privacy concerns, so this feature will be careful: likely opt-in
or only metadata by default. Even without full payload logs, showing error messages from failed
requests in the console is helpful (so devs can correct mistakes without digging blindly).
Support & Community: The platform will include links to support resources. This includes a

discuss (depending on our strategy). Enterprises will have a dedicated support contact or SLA,

status page (like status.convin.ai) so users can check if there are any service outages or
maintenance – transparency builds trust, especially for enterprise clients (Deepgram has a status
page link on their site ).

Innovative Features and Differentiators
While the core features align with industry leaders, Convin’s platform will also introduce innovations that
set it apart from existing STT/TTS services. These are features inspired by emerging user needs and gaps in
current offerings, aimed to provide unique value:
LLM-Assisted Transcription & Insights: Leveraging Convin’s in-house LLM, we plan to offer AI post￾processing on transcripts that competitors don’t provide out-of-the-box. For example, after
obtaining a raw transcript via STT, an optional step can run the transcript through our LLM to
produce a summary, action items, or sentiment analysis of the conversation. This could be
exposed as an additional API (e.g. a “Conversation Insights API”) or a parameter on the STT request
( ?summary=true ). In practical terms, a call center could send audio and get not just the transcript
but also an automatic call summary and key highlights, saving agent time (a feature aligned with
Convin’s conversation intelligence product). This goes beyond what pure STT services do, moving
into the realm of contact center AI where LLMs summarize calls . We could cite that “custom
call summarization” is a huge time-saver in contact centers . This feature is relatively low￾effort since we already have an LLM – it’s about packaging it for users. It’s a major differentiator for
enterprise clients who can get transcription + summarization in one step.
Real-time Agent Assist Hooks: Extending the above, in streaming mode we could allow real-time

protect their data. Our aim is to deliver a platform that works as good as it looks – fast, secure, and scalable.
Conclusion
The Convin Voice API Platform PRD outlines a comprehensive product that, if executed well, will establish
Convin as a serious player in the voice AI domain. By focusing initially on core STT and TTS capabilities and
doing them exceptionally – with accuracy, low latency, multi-language support, and high-quality voices – we
address the fundamental needs that platforms like Deepgram and ElevenLabs have validated. Layering on
top a superior developer experience (intuitive console, great docs, free trials) and enterprise features
(flexible deployment, custom models, strong security) gives us competitive parity and in many cases an
edge. Finally, our innovative features (LLM-powered summarizations, voice cloning, integrated insights)
give the platform a unique character and potential to lead the industry rather than just follow.
Success will be measured by adoption (developers signing up, enterprises in pilot), by the quality metrics
(e.g., transcription accuracy against benchmarks, voice naturalness feedback), and by the platform’s stability
in real-world use. We expect to launch with a compelling MVP that includes all “usable and noteworthy
features which can be shipped fast” – meaning the essentials and a few delights – and then iterate rapidly.
The design will draw users in, but the reliability and accuracy will keep them. By adhering to this PRD, the
team has a clear blueprint: deliver a cloud voice AI platform that is powerful yet easy, innovative yet
practical, and appealing to both startups and enterprises alike. With Convin’s LLM expertise and
commitment to quality, this platform can become the go-to choice for anyone looking to add speech
capabilities to their products, much like how Deepgram is lauded for STT or ElevenLabs for TTS –
except we’ll offer the best of both in one unified solution.
